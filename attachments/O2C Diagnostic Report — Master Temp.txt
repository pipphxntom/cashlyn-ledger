O2C Diagnostic Report — Master Template

The deliverable you hand to the CFO after 2 weeks


How to use this template

This is your diagnostic report skeleton. For each client, you fill it with THEIR numbers,
THEIR process, THEIR pain. The structure stays the same; the content is 100% custom.
A good diagnostic report does three things:


Quantifies the problem in rupees — not "your DSO is high" but "₹3.7 crore is stuck beyond terms"
Shows them WHY — root causes they couldn't see from inside
Gives a clear, prioritized path out — what to fix first, second, third


The report is 12–18 pages. Not a 50-page novel. CFOs don't read long reports — they read
sharp ones. Every page must earn its place.

Page 2–3 — Current State: Your O2C Process Map

What this section contains

A visual flowchart of the client's actual invoice-to-cash process, as you observed it.
Not what their SOP says — what actually happens.

How to build this per client


Interview 3–5 people: the billing/invoicing person, 1–2 collectors, the AR manager, and the controller/CFO.
Walk the actual process: Invoice generation → approval → delivery → customer receipt confirmation → payment follow-up → cash application → reconciliation.
Mark every handoff, delay point, and decision fork.
Highlight the gaps between "what should happen" and "what actually happens."

Fields needed: Customer name, invoice number, invoice date, due date, amount, amount paid, balance, aging bucket, business unit/segment (if available)

2. DSO calculation & decomposition


Actual DSO: [X] days
Best Possible DSO (BPDSO): [Y] days (= Current receivables ÷ daily sales)
Average Days Delinquent (ADD): [X – Y] days (= the gap that's process-driven)
Dollar value of the gap: ADD × daily revenue = ₹[Z] trapped

Top 10 customers by overdue amount (with days delinquent for each)
What % of total overdue do the top 10/20 represent?
Are there "chronic late payers" vs. "one-time issues"?

For each major finding, use this format:


Finding [#]: [One-line description]

What we observed: [2–3 sentences describing the specific issue, with data]

Why it matters: [Quantify the impact in ₹ or days]

Root cause: [Why this is happening — process gap, system limitation, staffing, etc.]

Recommended fix: [Specific action, not generic advice]

Estimated impact: [₹ freed or DSO days reduced]

Effort level: Low / Medium / High

Timeline: Immediate / 30 days / 60 days / 90 days



Aim for 5–8 findings. More than 8 overwhelms; fewer than 5 feels thin.

CUSTOMIZATION PRINCIPLES

Principle 1: Lead with THEIR number, not your framework

Every client gets a different "headline number" on page 1. For a manufacturer it might
be ₹6 crore stuck. For a SaaS company it might be 42 extra DSO days. Find the number
that will make THIS CFO's eyes widen, and build the entire report around it.

Principle 2: Match the language to the industry


Manufacturing: talk about "working capital," "inventory funding," "supplier terms"
SaaS: talk about "cash runway," "net dollar retention impact," "monthly burn"
Services: talk about "project-to-cash cycle," "unbilled receivables," "WIP aging"
Distribution: talk about "inventory turns," "margin compression from float"


Principle 3: Calibrate depth to the audience


If presenting to the CFO alone: keep it strategic, heavy on ₹ impact, light on process detail
If presenting to CFO + AR team: balance strategic with operational, include the process map
If presenting to the AR manager (who will champion it upward): heavy on operational fixes, give them ammunition to sell the retainer to their boss


Principle 4: Always include something they didn't expect

The findings they already know ("yes, our DSO is high") build credibility. The one finding
they DIDN'T know ("40% of your disputes are caused by invoice timing, not invoice accuracy")
is what closes the deal. Dig for the non-obvious.

Principle 5: Make the quick wins genuinely quick

If your "quick wins" require a 2-week project, they're not quick wins. They should be
things the client can do THIS WEEK. The faster they see a result, the faster the retainer
conversation happens.

Principle 6: Price the diagnostic relative to the problem


Small company (₹50–100Cr revenue): ₹50,000–75,000
Mid company (₹100–300Cr revenue): ₹75,000–1,25,000
Larger mid-market (₹300–500Cr revenue): ₹1,25,000–2,00,000


Never price it below ₹50,000. Below that, it signals "not serious" and attracts
clients who won't pay for the retainer either.

Principle 7: The report is a sales document disguised as a diagnostic

Every page should make the reader think: "We need help implementing this."
The roadmap should be clear enough that they COULD do it themselves, but detailed
enough that they realize they probably won't without external help. That tension is
what converts diagnostics into retainers.
